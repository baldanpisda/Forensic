An easy way to map crime scenes, skulls, and faces using the iPhone Pro and iPad Pro tablet's 3D Lidar camera scanner. Furthermore, about the pros and cons of using it in forensics. 
What is LIDAR scanner?
LiDAR can perform tasks even at night without requiring an external light source. LiDAR tends to provide more precise and accurate scanning compared to photogrammetry. Lidar can give you detailed information on even small areas. A great example of this is electrical cables. You can use LiDAR to map overhead lines in detail and analyze power line sags and safety.

Disadvantages of LiDAR
The most obvious obstacle to working with LiDAR is its cost. Lidar hardware may also be difficult to configure. Difficult to use is not common. When the data collected by LiDAR is not photographed, it is difficult to read and analyze visual details about the environment.
Make a description of the crime scene
Today, crime scene investigators with the U.S. Bureau of Investigation and police and state police are using 3D laser scanners to describe and describe detailed, data-driven crime scenes to bring justice to victims while increasing public and legal oversight. 3D laser scanning technology is used by investigators and experts to reconstruct crime scenes and accurately depict the relationship of each piece of evidence. It is a powerful investigative tool, especially when the legal system uses it as high-tech evidence in the courtroom.

 
Figure 2. Crime scene imaging using LiDAR scanners.
This scan is important to investigators because they can accurately locate evidence, restore the crime scene to its final state, and reconstruct the circumstances that led up to it. The result is a more comprehensive investigation that goes beyond the capabilities of traditional forensic tools or the talents of the people working to describe the scene. As the adoption of high-tech tools in the courtroom increases, 3D scanning may become standard forensic practice.

Using iPhone and iPad LIDAR Scanners for Forensic Imaging of Crime and Accident Scenes, Cranial Imaging and Profile Analysis
Documenting crime and accident scenes in three dimensions (3D) is a common and increasingly common practice today.
At the scene of the accident, such documentation is usually carried out by specially trained people, using various 3D imaging devices and methods, such as terrestrial laser scanners.
